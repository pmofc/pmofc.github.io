10 Side Hustles You Can Start Today With $0

Most “make money” lists are secretly sales pitches for a course. This one isn't. Everything below costs literally $0 to start — no equipment beyond a phone or a laptop you already own, no “starter kits,” no subscription you must buy first. Pay rates are realistic ranges people commonly report, not dream numbers.

In this guide

  1. Freelance a skill you already have
  2. Sell things you already own
  3. Tutor or teach conversational English
  4. Get paid to test websites
  5. Print-on-demand design
  6. Content creation (faceless works)
  7. Virtual assistant work
  8. AI training & data annotation
  9. Sell digital templates
  10. Local services, zero overhead
  11. Three rules that protect your money

1. Freelance a skill you already have

Writing, video editing, graphic design, translation, spreadsheets, voice-over — platforms like Upwork, Fiverr and Freelancer are free to join and take a cut only when you get paid. Start by doing small jobs cheaply to collect reviews, then raise your rates every 5–10 completed projects.

  • Realistic pay: $5–25/hour at the start; $30–75+/hour once you have reviews and a niche.
  • Fastest start: free tools do the heavy lifting — Canva Free for design, CapCut for video editing, Google Docs for writing.

2. Sell things you already own

The classic zero-capital starter: list unused clothes, electronics, books and furniture on Facebook Marketplace, Vinted, eBay or OfferUp. Local pickup means no shipping cost; you convert clutter into your first cash cushion, which you can reinvest into higher-margin flipping later.

💡 Key move: search sold listings for your item before pricing it — price at 60–70% of what actually sold this week, not what hopeful sellers are asking.

3. Tutor or teach conversational English

If you're fluent in English (or good at math, music, or coding homework), platforms like Preply, Cambly, Wyzant or italki let you create a profile for free and set your own rate. No teaching certificate required for conversational practice on many platforms.

  • Realistic pay: $8–25/hour for conversation practice; more for exam prep or technical subjects.
  • Tip: a 60-second intro video triples bookings on most platforms — record it with your phone.

4. Get paid to test websites and apps

Companies pay real users to click through their sites and think out loud. UserTesting, Userlytics and TryMyUI-style platforms pay per 10–20 minute test. Payouts are small but immediate, and no skill is needed beyond honest narration.

  • Realistic pay: $4–$10 per test; $50–$200/month as a side trickle, not a salary.
  • Reality check: tests are intermittent — apply to 3–4 panels at once so your dashboard stays busy.

5. Print-on-demand design

Printful, Printify and Redbubble are free to join: you upload designs, they print and ship on demand, and you keep the margin. Zero inventory, zero upfront cost. Simple text-based and niche-humor designs (for teachers, nurses, runners, dog breeds) consistently outsell generic art.

6. Content creation — faceless works fine

You don't need to show your face. Niche YouTube channels, TikToks and Pinterest accounts built around demonstrations, voiceover explainers, or text-on-screen tips earn through ad revenue, affiliate links and sponsorships. It's the slowest burner on this list — think months, not days — but the income compounds and keeps paying on old videos.

“The first 30 videos teach the algorithm who your audience is. Most people quit at video 7 — that's the real filter.”

7. Virtual assistant work

Solo business owners drown in inbox management, scheduling, research and social posting. A VA needs organizational skill more than technical skill. Look for openings on Upwork, OnlineJobs.ph and Balsify-style job boards, or pitch small businesses directly by email — a short, specific pitch (“I noticed your podcast has no show notes; I'll do the last 5 episodes free so you can see the quality”) beats a generic résumé.

  • Realistic pay: $6–15/hour starting; $20+/hour for specialized VAs (e.g., podcast or e-commerce).

8. AI training & data annotation

AI companies pay humans to rate and write example responses. DataAnnotation, Outlier and Appen-style projects are remote, flexible, and free to join. Screening tests take an hour or two and are unpaid — pass rate varies, so apply to several.

  • Realistic pay: $15–25/hour for English writing tasks; more for coding-related projects.

9. Sell digital templates

Make once, sell forever: resume templates, budget spreadsheets, Notion dashboards, Canva social-media kits. Gumroad and Payhip are free to start (they take a percentage per sale). Your first product can be the exact spreadsheet you already made for yourself.

10. Local services with zero overhead

Offline still beats online for fastest first dollar: dog walking, plant care, errand running, pressure washing (rent/borrow the machine for the first job and let the client's payment cover it). Post in local Facebook groups and Nextdoor — it's free advertising where people actually hire.

Three rules that protect your money

  1. Never pay to apply. A real opportunity pays you. Training fees, “background check” fees and starter kits are the #1 scam pattern.
  2. Track your hourly rate. Log hours vs. earnings in a free Notion or Google Sheet for two weeks — drop anything paying under $8/hour that can't scale.
  3. Pick one, give it 30 days. Switching hustles every weekend is why most people earn $0. Depth beats variety.

Next step: pick exactly one from this list, block 60 minutes today, and take the smallest possible action — create the profile, list one item, or send three pitch emails. Momentum is the whole game.
