AdSense Approval Checklist: Get Approved on Your First Try

Most AdSense rejections aren't bad luck — they're predictable. Google's reviewers (human + automated) check a known list of things, and this page is that list. Work through it before applying and your odds improve dramatically.

In this guide

  1. Account & site eligibility
  2. Content requirements
  3. The three pages reviewers demand
  4. The code you must have live
  5. Top rejection reasons & fixes
  6. After approval: first ad units

✅ Account & site eligibility

  • You're 18+ with one AdSense account (one per person, enforced).
  • You own the site or have full access to its code (free self-hosted sites qualify — that's what this template is for).
  • Your site is live and indexed: visit site:yourdomain.com on Google. If nothing appears, submit it in Search Console and wait a week or two.
  • Note on free subdomains: custom domains are strongly preferred; Google often rejects unknown free subdomains. The exception is .blogspot.com (Blogger), which is allowed since Google owns it.
  • The site is 3+ weeks old in practice (not an official rule, but newer sites are frequently declined with “under construction”).

✅ Content requirements

  • 20–30 posts of original, helpful content. Thin, copied, auto-generated or “spun” content is the #1 rejection cause (“low value content”).
  • Posts are 800+ words on average, with headings, images and real information.
  • Clear structure: categories/menus so reviewers can find your work.
  • No prohibited content: no adult material, violent or hateful content, copyright-infringing media (use Unsplash/Pexels), or misleading claims.
  • Traffic isn't officially required — but a trickle of real visitors proves your site is alive. Post consistently for 3–4 weeks before applying.

✅ The three pages reviewers demand

  1. About — who runs the site and why. A name and face helps.
  2. Contact — a working form or email address.
  3. Privacy policy — must state you use cookies and third-party advertising (this template ships with one: privacy policy).
💡 This template already includes all three pages, a cookie consent banner, and an ads.txt file — the technical requirements are done for you.

✅ The code you must have live

When you apply, AdSense gives you a verification script. It goes in the <head> of every page. In this template, search the files for ADSENSE SLOT — each marks a ready spot:

<script async
  src="https://pagead2.googlesyndication.com/pagead/js/adsbygoogle.js?client=ca-pub-XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX"
  crossorigin="anonymous"></script>

Also add the ads.txt line AdSense shows you (file already exists at the site root — replace the placeholder). Missing ads.txt causes “ads.txt status” warnings later, so do it on day one.

Top rejection reasons & fixes

Rejection message Real fix
“Low value content” Delete thin/AI-spun posts; write 10 deep, original articles; apply again in 2–3 weeks.
“Site down or unavailable” Check hosting uptime, robots.txt isn't blocking Google, site loads without errors.
“Under construction” Fill empty nav items, remove “coming soon” pages, finish the about/contact pages.
“Policy violation” Audit against prohibited content; remove scraped text/images and copyright music.
“Unsupported language/domain” Custom domain usually resolves this; check AdSense's supported-language list.

After approval: your first ad units

  1. Create a responsive display ad and an in-article ad in AdSense → Ads → By ad unit.
  2. Paste each snippet where this template's placeholders sit (marked ADSENSE SLOT: header, in-article ×2, sidebar, footer leaderboard).
  3. Enable Auto ads (experiments) cautiously — manual placements usually look better and load faster.
  4. Never click your own ads and never ask anyone to — that's the fastest account ban in the industry.

If AdSense keeps rejecting you despite a solid site, alternatives in the meantime: Ezoic (no minimum traffic), Media.net, or going affiliate-first — which often pays better than ads at low traffic anyway. See highest-paying niches to make every future visitor count.
