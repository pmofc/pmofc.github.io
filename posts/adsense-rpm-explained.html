AdSense RPM Explained: What Blogs Really Earn Per 1,000 Visitors

“How much does AdSense pay?” is the most-asked, worst-answered question in blogging. The honest answer is a range — and once you understand RPM, you can predict your own earnings with a simple multiplication. Let's make the math concrete.

In this guide

  1. What RPM actually means
  2. Realistic RPM ranges by niche
  3. The math you can actually use
  4. The 5 factors that move your RPM
  5. How to raise yours

What RPM actually means

RPM = revenue ÷ sessions × 1,000. If 5,000 people visited your blog this month and you earned $60, your RPM is $12. It's not the same as CPC (what one click pays) — RPM blends clicks, view-through earnings, ad coverage and traffic quality into the one number that actually predicts your paycheck.

Realistic RPM ranges by niche

These are commonly reported ranges for organic traffic from high-value countries (US/UK/CA/AU). Treat them as honest ballparks, not promises:

Niche Typical RPM
Finance, insurance, legal $15–40+
Software / SaaS / hosting $8–20
Education, careers, B2B $6–15
Health, home, travel $4–12
Food, crafts, general lifestyle $2–6
Entertainment, memes, celebrities $1–3

See the highest-paying niches breakdown for why this spread exists.

The math you can actually use

📊 Your monthly ad earnings ≈ monthly sessions × RPM ÷ 1,000.
10,000 visits × $12 RPM = $120/month. Same traffic in a finance niche at $25 RPM = $250/month. Same traffic in memes at $2 = $20/month. Traffic is only half the equation — niche is the multiplier.

The 5 factors that move your RPM

  1. Audience geography. A US/UK visitor is worth 3–10× a visitor from low-ad-bid markets. This is the single biggest hidden variable.
  2. Niche intent. Readers about to buy something (loans, software, insurance) attract premium advertisers.
  3. Seasonality. Q4 (Oct–Dec) is peak ad spending; January dips. Don't panic at a February drop.
  4. Traffic source. Search visitors read and click; social drive-by visitors rarely do. Organic-heavy blogs earn more per visit.
  5. Placement & layout. In-content ads near relevant sections outperform cluttered sidebars. Fewer, well-placed units usually beat many bad ones.

How to raise yours

  • Publish more pages targeting commercial keywords (“best X for Y”, “X vs Y”, “X review”).
  • Internally link every article toward your money pages — pass visitors deeper into the site.
  • Write for countries with high ad demand if it fits your content naturally (English helps).
  • Once traffic crosses ~10k sessions/month, test premium ad networks (Ezoic, Monumetric, Mediavine at higher tiers) which often double AdSense RPMs.
  • Track RPM monthly in a spreadsheet so you know if a change helped.
