How to Start a Blog for Free in 2026 (and Actually Monetize It)

Blogging is still one of the few online income streams where the required investment is time, not money. The catch: most new blogs fail because they skip the boring parts. This guide walks through all of them — choosing a niche you can dominate, picking a free platform without shooting yourself in the foot, building a content plan, and monetizing in the order that actually works.

Step 1: Pick a niche with buyers in it

The intersection you want is: a topic you can write 100 posts about × people actively searching for it × advertisers willing to pay for those readers. Personal finance, software reviews, home improvement and education score high on the third point (see the highest-paying AdSense niches), but you'll burn out writing about insurance if you hate it.

  • Write down 3 topics you could talk about for a year without research fatigue.
  • For each, Google your 10 post ideas — if the first page is only giant brands, narrow further (e.g., “personal finance” → “budgeting for freelancers with irregular income”).
  • Niche sub-topics win. Small fish, small pond, big share.

Step 2: Choose a free platform — the real trade-offs

Platform Best for Watch out
WordPress.com (free) Long-term blogging, huge ecosystem Free plan shows their ads, not yours; custom domain costs money later
Blogger AdSense from day one, easiest approval path Dated design, less control
GitHub Pages + Netlify (static site) Full control, fast, literally $0 forever You edit HTML or use a generator — steeper start
Medium / Substack Zero setup, built-in audience You don't own the platform; weak for AdSense

Recommendation: if your end goal is AdSense, a site you own (Blogger or a static site like this one) beats writing only on Medium. You can migrate later, but URLs and backlinks you build early are worth keeping.

Step 3: The three pages AdSense demands

Before writing article #10, set up these — reviewers check for them:

  1. About page — who writes this, why you're qualified.
  2. Contact page — a working form or email.
  3. Privacy policy — must mention cookies and third-party advertising (copy ours as a base: privacy policy template).

Step 4: A content plan that beats 90% of blogs

  • 20–30 posts before you apply to AdSense. Each 800–1,500 words, genuinely helpful, no AI-generated filler — reviewers reject thin content fast.
  • Pillar + spoke structure: one big guide (“How to start freelancing”) linking to 8–10 narrow posts (“Upwork profile examples”, “first invoice template”). Google loves the structure; readers stay longer.
  • Answer the question in the first 100 words, then expand. Google's featured snippets reward this; so do impatient readers.
  • Free keyword research: type your topic into Google and harvest the autocomplete suggestions and the “People also ask” boxes. That's the search demand map, no tool needed.

Step 5: Get traffic without spending

  1. Google Search Console (free, essential): register your site the day it's live. It tells you which queries you're ranking 8–20 for — those are your next posts' titles.
  2. Pinterest (underrated for blogs): it's a visual search engine, not social media. Five pins per post, keyworded titles.
  3. Reddit & forums — the 90/10 rule: 90% genuinely helpful answers, 10% “I wrote a fuller guide here.” Self-promotion-first accounts get banned and deserve it.
  4. Internal links: every new post links to 2–3 older ones. This is the highest-leverage 5 minutes in blogging.

Step 6: Monetize in the right order

  1. Affiliate links first (day one). No approval process, no traffic minimums. Write honest reviews of free tools you actually use and join their programs (most SaaS affiliate programs pay 20–30% recurring). Always disclose, and use rel="sponsored nofollow".
  2. AdSense second (month 2–4). Apply after you have 20–30 solid posts + the three legal pages. Full checklist: AdSense approval checklist.
  3. Your own digital product third (month 4+). A $9 template or checklist converts far better than ads once you know what readers struggle with (they'll tell you in comments and emails).
  4. Sponsorships last. Even 5,000 monthly visitors can sell a $50–150 sponsored post in buyer-heavy niches.

The honest timeline

Months 1–2: zero visitors, publish 2–3 posts a week. Months 3–5: first search impressions trickle in; apply to AdSense. Months 6–9: first $10–100/month as rankings settle. Month 12: $100–500/month is a realistic median for consistent, quality-focused bloggers in a commercial niche. Most people who “failed” quit at month 4 — the ones who win just didn't.
